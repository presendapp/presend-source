Common uses

Email attachment limits (often 10-25MB), job application portals with strict upload caps, faster loading for PDFs embedded on a website, and freeing up storage on a phone or shared drive are the most common reasons people need a smaller PDF without losing the document's content.

What actually makes PDFs large

Embedded fonts, uncompressed images, and leftover editing history from tools like Word or Photoshop are the usual culprits — not the text itself, which takes up very little space. Optimizing structure and re-compressing images typically shrinks a file significantly without visibly affecting quality.

FAQ

Will compression reduce text quality or make it blurry?
No. Text remains sharp — compression targets embedded images and internal structure, not the text layer itself.

How much smaller will my file get?
It depends heavily on content. PDFs with large images often shrink the most; text-only documents may see smaller gains since there's less to compress.

Is my document uploaded to a server?
No. Compression happens entirely in your browser — the file never leaves your device.
